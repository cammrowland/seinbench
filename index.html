Benchmark · Comedy · Nothing

Can a language model write a show about nothing?

SeinBench gives every frontier model the same task: pitch an original Seinfeld episode and write one scene. A panel of one expert judge then awards a SeinScore from 0 to 100, which places it in one of five rigorously defined tiers, from "It Stinks!" to "Gold, Jerry! Gold!"

The Scale

Every SeinScore from 0 to 100 falls into one of five tiers, validated against zero external benchmarks.

Methodology

Our evaluation protocol, written up with the gravity it deserves.

Task

One fixed prompt asks for an original episode: a title, a one-paragraph synopsis covering all four leads' storylines, and one scene in script format.

Sampling

One attempt per model, temperature 1.0, through OpenRouter. No retries and no best-of-n. You get one shot. No soup for you.

Model selection

Only major releases are tested, picked by hand when something new comes out. Minor variants, distillations, and models with tiny names don't get in.

Evaluation

A single human expert who has seen every episode several times fills in two scorecards. The synopsis is rated on originality, plot weaving and character authenticity; the scene on laughs, voices and craft. These produce a suggested SeinScore, to which the judge applies a discretionary nudge, as is customary in rigorous science. Inter-rater agreement is 100%, which our statisticians call "suspicious."

Contamination

Models may have memorized real episodes. Recycling a real plot is treated the way Jerry treats a re-gifted label maker.

Known limitations

A sample size of one. The judge is biased and has strong opinions about the finale. The results are not reproducible, and frankly not even producible.
